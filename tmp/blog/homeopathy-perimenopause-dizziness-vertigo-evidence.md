## Why Does Dizziness Happen in Perimenopause?

**Fluctuating oestrogen affects more of your body's balance systems than people expect.** It plays a role in blood vessel tone and blood pressure regulation, meaning sudden drops in blood pressure on standing (sometimes called orthostatic dizziness) can become more noticeable during perimenopause. Oestrogen receptors are also present in the inner ear, the structure responsible for your sense of balance, which is one proposed reason some women notice new or worsening vertigo-type sensations during this transition.

**Anxiety and poor sleep, both common in perimenopause**, can also contribute to a generalised feeling of unsteadiness or lightheadedness that isn't true vertigo (a spinning sensation) but feels similarly unsettling and is often described using the same words.

## What Do Women Commonly Try in Homeopathy?

**A homeopathic approach to dizziness is individualised rather than standardised.** A practitioner typically asks detailed questions about exactly how the dizziness presents, whether it's worse on standing, lying down, or turning your head, whether it comes with nausea, and what seems to trigger or ease it, before selecting a remedy intended to match that specific pattern. This is a fundamentally different approach from conventional medicine's focus on identifying and treating an underlying cause.

**It's worth being direct about what this means**: homeopathy, by its own internal logic, is not aimed at correcting the hormonal fluctuation or ruling out other causes of dizziness, it is aimed at addressing the symptom pattern as the practitioner has understood it. This distinction matters when deciding what role, if any, it should play alongside proper medical evaluation.

## What Does the Evidence Actually Show?

**High-quality, rigorous scientific evidence specifically testing homeopathy for dizziness or vertigo is limited.** Where studies exist, they are often small, carry methodological limitations, or lack the kind of blinding and controls needed to separate a genuine treatment effect from the placebo effect or from dizziness naturally fluctuating over time.

**The broader scientific evidence base for homeopathy remains genuinely contested.** Major systematic reviews and health bodies in multiple countries have concluded that homeopathy's effects are not reliably distinguishable from placebo across the conditions studied, a position homeopathy's practitioners and proponents dispute. This is worth knowing honestly rather than glossing over, whichever way you personally lean on the question.

**None of this means dizziness itself isn't real, or that your experience of relief, if you feel it, isn't genuine to you.** It means the scientific case for homeopathy specifically causing that relief, as opposed to the dizziness easing on its own, a placebo effect, or another change you made around the same time, is not well established.

## Why This Symptom Specifically Needs Medical Evaluation First

**Dizziness has several possible causes beyond perimenopausal hormonal change**, and this is the single most important point in this entire piece. Low blood pressure, anaemia (common in women with heavy perimenopausal bleeding), inner ear conditions like benign positional vertigo, medication side effects, low blood sugar, and, less commonly but importantly, cardiac arrhythmias or neurological causes can all present as dizziness.

**A doctor can run simple, accessible checks**, blood pressure in different positions, a basic blood count to check for anaemia, and further tests if your history suggests something beyond the usual pattern, to establish what's actually driving your dizziness before you or anyone else attributes it confidently to "just perimenopause."

**Certain features mean you should seek care urgently**, including dizziness with chest pain, slurred speech, one-sided weakness, severe headache, or fainting, since these combinations can indicate a cardiac or neurological emergency rather than a routine hormonal symptom. In an emergency, call 112.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can homeopathy cure perimenopausal dizziness?**
There is no high-quality scientific evidence confirming this, and the broader evidence base for homeopathy remains contested. It should never be used as a substitute for ruling out other, sometimes more serious, causes of dizziness.

**Is it safe to try a homeopathic remedy alongside seeing my doctor?**
Generally yes, classical homeopathic remedies in standard dilutions are not known to interact dangerously with conventional medication, but you should still tell your doctor you're using one, so your full picture is on record and nothing is missed or misattributed.

**My dizziness improved after starting a homeopathic remedy. Doesn't that prove it worked?**
Not necessarily. Dizziness often fluctuates naturally, and improvement after starting something doesn't prove that thing caused the improvement, this is exactly why controlled trials, which separate a real effect from natural fluctuation and placebo response, matter for answering the question properly.

**When should I actually worry about dizziness in perimenopause?**
If it's new, worsening, happens with chest pain, fainting, slurred speech, or one-sided weakness, or is affecting your safety (for instance, while driving or on stairs), see a doctor promptly rather than waiting to see if a remedy helps first.

**Where can I get a proper evaluation?**
Start with your gynaecologist or a general physician, who can check your blood pressure, order a basic blood count, and refer you further if needed.
