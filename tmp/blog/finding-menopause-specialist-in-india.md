Your regular gynaecologist has delivered your babies, managed your PCOS in your twenties, maybe even treated a fibroid or two. But when you bring up perimenopause, hormone therapy, or a symptom that feels bigger than "just adjust and manage," the conversation goes strangely thin. This is common, and it does not mean your gynaecologist is a bad doctor, it usually means menopause care simply was not a major part of their training, the way it was not a major part of most Indian medical education until fairly recently. Finding a doctor who actually specialises in this life stage takes a bit more effort, but it is very possible, and it changes the entire experience of getting care.

## What Makes a Doctor a "Menopause Specialist," Specifically?

There is no single official title in India that guarantees expertise, which is part of why this search feels harder than it should. In practice, look for a gynaecologist who has pursued additional certification or fellowship training focused on menopause, is affiliated with or certified by a national or international menopause society, and whose practice genuinely includes a significant number of perimenopausal and menopausal patients rather than this being a small part of a broader obstetrics-heavy practice. **None of this means your existing gynaecologist cannot become a good resource**, some are genuinely excellent and simply need you to raise the topic directly, but if a first attempt at that conversation has gone nowhere, a dedicated specialist is worth actively seeking out.

## What to Expect From a Good First Appointment

**A detailed history, not just a form.** A specialist will typically ask about your cycle pattern over the last year, the full range of your symptoms including ones you might not think to mention like brain fog or joint pain, your family history, and your personal priorities, before jumping to any test or prescription.

**A real conversation about options, including hormone therapy.** You should leave the appointment understanding what your choices actually are, not just being told to "wait it out" or handed a prescription without explanation.

**Willingness to discuss risk in your specific context**, not generic caution. A good specialist explains how your personal history, not a blanket rule, shapes whether hormone therapy or another approach makes sense for you.

**A plan for follow-up.** Perimenopause care is rarely a single-visit fix; a specialist should outline what to expect over the coming months and when to come back.

## Questions Worth Asking Before You Book

**"Do you regularly manage perimenopause as a significant part of your practice?"** This single question filters out a lot.

**"Do you prescribe hormone therapy, and under what circumstances would you or wouldn't you recommend it?"** A specialist should be able to answer this clearly and specifically, not vaguely.

**"What is the consultation fee, and are follow-ups included or charged separately?"** Costs vary considerably, and it is fair to ask upfront.

## In the Indian Context

**Cost and access vary enormously by city.** A consultation with a menopause specialist at a private hospital in a metro can range from roughly ₹1,000 to ₹3,000 for a first visit, with follow-ups sometimes lower; public hospital gynaecology departments in many cities also see perimenopausal patients, though wait times can be longer and dedicated menopause expertise is less consistent. If cost is a genuine barrier, it is worth asking directly about public hospital options in your city, since several government medical colleges do run gynaecology departments capable of managing perimenopause competently, even without a "menopause clinic" label.

**Family dynamics can complicate the search itself.** Some women describe needing to justify a dedicated specialist visit to family as "necessary" in a way a general check-up never requires, particularly in joint-family households where symptoms like mood changes or low libido are minimised as "normal at this age." You do not need anyone's permission to seek out the right doctor for you, and framing it simply as a routine health appointment, without over-explaining, is a reasonable way to navigate this if needed.

**Tier-2 and tier-3 cities have fewer dedicated options**, but this gap is narrowing, and video consultations with specialists based in larger cities have made this more workable than it was even a few years ago.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is a menopause specialist always a gynaecologist?**
Almost always, yes, though endocrinologists can also be involved in specific cases, particularly where other hormonal conditions overlap. For most women, a gynaecologist with menopause-focused training is the right starting point.

**Do I need a referral to see a menopause specialist in India?**
No, in most cases you can book directly with a specialist or clinic without a referral from another doctor, though checking with the specific hospital is worth doing as policies vary.

**What if there is no dedicated menopause clinic in my city?**
Ask your regular gynaecologist directly whether they manage perimenopause regularly, or look into video consultation options with specialists in larger nearby cities. Community recommendations, including from online forums for Indian women, can also point you toward doctors with strong reputations even without a formal "menopause clinic" title.

**How do I know if I actually need a specialist versus my regular gynaecologist?**
If your regular gynaecologist engages seriously with your symptoms, discusses treatment options including hormone therapy where relevant, and you feel properly heard, they may be entirely sufficient. A specialist becomes more valuable when your symptoms are complex, your first conversation felt dismissive, or you specifically want a detailed discussion of hormone therapy.
