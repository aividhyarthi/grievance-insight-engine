If you grew up in an Indian household, there is a decent chance someone, a grandmother, a mother, a neighbourhood aunty, has pressed a bottle of dark, almost black hair oil into your hands at some point and told you it was good for your hair. That oil was very likely bhringraj, one of the most widely used Ayurvedic herbs for hair in the country. When perimenopausal hair thinning starts, the handful that collects in the drain after a shower, the widening part, the eyebrow and eyelash thinning that nobody warns you about, many women reach straight back for it, half out of habit and half out of hope. Whether it actually does anything for hormonally driven hair loss is a separate, more careful question.

## What is bhringraj, and why is it so tied to hair in Indian households

**Bhringraj**, also called "false daisy" (Eclipta alba, sometimes Eclipta prostrata), is a small herb that grows widely across India and is dried, powdered, or infused into oil for use on hair and scalp. In Ayurveda, it is considered a rasayana for hair, something believed to nourish and rejuvenate, and it is one of the most commonly recommended herbs specifically for premature greying, hair fall, and scalp dryness. It is rarely sold or used on its own; most commercial bottles are a bhringraj-infused coconut or sesame oil base, often combined with other herbs like amla or brahmi.

**The appeal during perimenopause is obvious.** Hair thinning is one of the more quietly distressing symptoms of this transition, less talked about than hot flashes but just as capable of denting your confidence. A herb that is gentle, familiar, and already sitting in most Indian bathroom cabinets is an easy, low-risk thing to reach for before anything else.

## What does the actual research show

**The honest picture is mixed and still developing.** A handful of smaller studies, several conducted on animal models rather than large human trials, have looked at bhringraj's effect on hair growth and found some encouraging signals, including faster hair growth cycles and improved follicle activity in those limited settings. There is also laboratory evidence that bhringraj extract has antioxidant and some anti-inflammatory properties, which could theoretically support scalp health.

**What is missing is large, well-designed human clinical trials specifically on perimenopausal or hormonally driven hair thinning.** Most of what exists studies bhringraj for general hair fall or greying, not the oestrogen-related hair changes that show up during this particular life stage. This does not mean it does nothing, but it means the confident claims you will see on product packaging ("clinically proven," "doubles hair growth") usually outpace what the actual published evidence supports.

## Why perimenopausal hair thinning is a different problem than bhringraj alone can solve

**Hormonal hair thinning during perimenopause has a specific mechanism.** As oestrogen levels fluctuate and decline, hair follicles that were previously supported by oestrogen can shift into a more androgen-sensitive pattern, leading to diffuse thinning, particularly at the crown and part line, that looks different from patchy hair loss. A topical oil, however good for the scalp, cannot change circulating hormone levels or follicle sensitivity to androgens.

**Other common contributors need to be ruled out first.** Thyroid dysfunction, which becomes more common around this age, iron deficiency from years of heavy perimenopausal bleeding, and chronic stress can all cause or worsen hair thinning, and all of these have their own specific treatments that a herbal oil will not substitute for. If your hair loss has been sudden, patchy, or associated with other symptoms like fatigue or irregular periods, a basic blood panel checking thyroid function and ferritin is worth asking your doctor for before you assume it is simply "hormones."

## In the Indian context

**India's herbal hair-care market is enormous and largely unregulated at the quality-control level.** A bottle labelled "bhringraj oil" can range from a well-formulated extract to one with a token amount of the actual herb diluted heavily into a base oil, and there is no simple way to verify this from the shelf. If you want to try it, buying from an established Ayurvedic brand or pharmacy with transparent sourcing, rather than an unbranded or very cheap option, at least improves your odds of getting a genuine product, though it still will not guarantee a clinical effect.

**It is also worth saying plainly to your family:** if an older relative insists bhringraj alone will fix noticeable hair thinning, it is reasonable to use the oil for scalp comfort while still seeing a doctor about the underlying cause, rather than choosing between the two.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can bhringraj oil regrow hair that has already thinned significantly?**
There is no strong clinical evidence that it reverses significant hair loss on its own. It may support scalp health and hair texture, but if thinning is substantial, a dermatologist can discuss evidence-based options alongside addressing any underlying hormonal or nutritional cause.

**Is it safe to use bhringraj oil alongside hormone therapy?**
Topical oils are generally low-risk since they are not absorbed systemically in meaningful amounts, but it is still worth mentioning to your doctor, especially if you are also taking any oral herbal supplement containing bhringraj rather than just using it as a hair oil.

**How long should I try it before deciding if it's working?**
Hair growth cycles are slow, so most traditional guidance suggests at least three to six months of consistent use before judging an effect, which is also roughly how long it takes to notice whether an underlying medical cause, once treated, is making a bigger difference.

**Should I see a doctor before trying it, or can I just start?**
If your hair loss is mild and gradual, there is little harm in trying a scalp oil while also booking a basic checkup. If it has been sudden, heavy, or patchy, see a doctor or dermatologist first rather than waiting to see if an oil helps.
