One practical piece rarely discussed alongside the medical side of perimenopause is cost. Understanding roughly what to expect, for consultations, tests, and treatment, helps you plan and budget rather than being caught off guard, and helps you make informed decisions about where to prioritise spending. Costs vary considerably by city, clinic, and individual circumstances, so the figures here are general guidance, not a fixed quote, but they give a realistic sense of what to expect.

## Doctor Consultations

**Gynaecologist consultations** in India typically range from roughly ₹500 to ₹2,000 or more per visit, depending on the city, the doctor's experience, and whether you see them at a government facility, a private clinic, or a corporate hospital. Government and public hospital consultations are often free or minimally priced, though with longer wait times and shorter individual appointments. As covered in our guide to getting your doctor to take perimenopause seriously, finding the right doctor matters enough that it is often worth the cost of a private consultation, at least for an initial, thorough assessment.

**Follow-up visits**, particularly if you start HRT or another treatment, are usually needed periodically, every few months initially, then less frequently, adding a recurring cost worth factoring into your planning.

## Diagnostic Tests

**Blood tests** are often central to properly evaluating perimenopause and ruling out overlapping conditions like thyroid problems, covered in our related guide. A fuller panel, potentially including hormone levels, thyroid function, vitamin D, iron studies, and a lipid profile, as covered in our cholesterol guide, might range from roughly ₹1,500 to ₹5,000 or more depending on how comprehensive it is and where it is done. Many diagnostic chains offer package deals for combined panels, often more economical than individual tests.

**Ultrasounds**, relevant to evaluating bleeding patterns as covered in our guide to perimenopausal bleeding evaluation, typically cost roughly ₹800 to ₹2,500.

**A bone density scan (DEXA)**, covered in our guide to understanding your results, generally costs somewhere in the range of ₹1,000 to ₹3,000, and is not always covered by routine health checkups unless specifically requested.

## Treatment Costs

**HRT costs vary considerably** depending on the specific form (patch, gel, tablet, as covered in our delivery methods guide), the brand, and whether generic options are available and suitable for you. This is worth discussing directly with your doctor, asking specifically whether a more affordable, equally effective option exists, since brand and formulation can meaningfully affect ongoing cost for a treatment you may take for an extended period.

**Vaginal moisturisers and local oestrogen**, covered in our guide to treating vaginal dryness, add their own ongoing cost, generally more modest than systemic HRT but still worth factoring into a regular budget if used long-term.

**Supplements**, as covered in our supplements guide, can add up if taken indiscriminately. Being selective, based on an actual identified need like a confirmed vitamin D deficiency, rather than taking a wide range speculatively, is both more effective and more cost-conscious.

## Making It More Manageable

**Ask about generic alternatives.** For both HRT and any other prescribed medication, generic versions are often significantly more affordable and, for most medications, equally effective, worth asking your doctor about directly.

**Consider government and public health facilities for routine needs.** While private care often offers more time and continuity, public facilities can meaningfully reduce cost for routine tests and follow-ups, particularly once you have an established diagnosis and treatment plan from an initial thorough private consultation.

**Check health insurance coverage.** Many health insurance policies in India cover consultations, diagnostic tests, and sometimes treatment related to perimenopause and menopause, though coverage varies significantly by policy. It is worth specifically checking what your policy covers, since this is not always intuitive from a policy summary alone.

**Bundle tests where sensible.** Rather than isolated, repeated single tests, discuss with your doctor whether a combined panel at one time is more cost-effective and gives a fuller picture at once.

**Prioritise based on impact, if budget is genuinely tight.** If cost is a real constraint, prioritise the assessment and treatment that addresses your most disruptive symptoms first, rather than attempting every possible test and treatment simultaneously. Your doctor can help you sequence this sensibly.

## When to See a Doctor

**Routine appointment**, and it is entirely reasonable to discuss cost openly with your doctor as part of planning your care, asking about more affordable equivalent options where they exist, and building a realistic, sustainable plan together.

Understanding the likely costs of perimenopause care ahead of time turns an unpredictable expense into something you can plan for, and having an open conversation with your doctor about cost is a normal, reasonable part of getting the care that is right for both your health and your budget.
