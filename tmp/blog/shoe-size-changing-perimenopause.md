You bought these sandals two years ago. They fit perfectly then. Now, by the time you've walked around all day, your feet have swelled enough that the straps leave marks, and you've started eyeing a size up when you buy new ones. If this sounds familiar, you are not imagining it, and it is not simply "getting older" in some vague, unexplainable way. Feet can genuinely change shape and size during perimenopause, for reasons that have everything to do with hormones, fluid balance, and the connective tissue holding your feet together.

<div class="tldr">
<p class="tldr-label">Quick Answer</p>
<p>Shifting oestrogen levels during perimenopause can cause fluid retention, weight changes, and loosening of the ligaments in your feet, all of which can genuinely change how your feet fit into shoes, sometimes temporarily by the day, sometimes more permanently over the years. It's a real, hormonally-linked change for many women, though sudden or one-sided swelling deserves medical attention rather than being assumed to be hormonal.</p>
<ul>
<li>Oestrogen affects fluid retention, which can make feet swell more, especially by evening or in heat</li>
<li>Falling oestrogen can also loosen ligaments, including in the feet, sometimes causing feet to widen or flatten slightly over time</li>
<li>Weight gain common in perimenopause adds load-bearing pressure that can also widen feet</li>
<li>Daily fluctuation (swelling by evening, normal by morning) is usually benign; sudden, one-sided, or painful swelling needs a doctor's assessment</li>
<li>Switching to properly fitted, slightly roomier footwear rather than forcing old shoes is usually more comfortable and reduces injury risk</li>

## Is It Normal for Feet to Actually Change Size, Not Just Swell Temporarily?

**Yes, both things can happen, and they're worth distinguishing.** Daily swelling, feet feeling tight by evening but back to normal by morning, is typically fluid-related and fluctuates with heat, salt intake, how long you've been standing, and where you are in your symptom pattern. A more permanent size change, needing a genuinely larger shoe size than you did five years ago, year-round, is more likely related to ligament laxity and weight changes gradually reshaping the foot's structure, specifically a slight widening or flattening of the arch. Podiatrists and researchers studying foot changes with age and hormonal shifts have documented this as a real, measurable phenomenon, not an old wives' tale, though it varies quite a bit between individuals in degree and timing.

## When Is Foot Swelling Something to Worry About?

**Daily, symmetrical swelling that resolves overnight is generally not a medical concern, but certain patterns deserve a doctor's attention.** Sudden swelling in one foot but not the other, swelling accompanied by pain, redness, or warmth, or swelling that doesn't improve with rest and elevation overnight can signal something other than hormonal fluid retention, such as a blood clot, an injury, or a circulation issue, and should be assessed promptly rather than assumed to be perimenopause. Swelling that comes on alongside shortness of breath or chest discomfort needs emergency care immediately. For most women, though, the day-to-day tightness in shoes by evening is a benign, if annoying, part of the hormonal fluctuation of this life stage.

## What Actually Helps?

**For daily fluid-related swelling, the same measures that help with general water retention tend to help feet specifically.** Reducing excess salt intake, staying well hydrated (counterintuitively, under-hydration can worsen fluid retention), elevating your feet when sitting for long periods, and avoiding standing still for very long stretches when possible all make a modest difference. For a more structural, long-term change in foot shape, the more realistic approach is accepting that your feet may genuinely need a different size or width now, rather than continuing to force them into shoes bought years ago. Getting properly measured at a shoe shop, rather than assuming your old size, and choosing shoes with some adjustability, laces, straps, or stretch panels, accommodates both the daily fluctuation and the longer-term change without needing multiple shoe wardrobes.

## In the Indian Context

**Traditional Indian footwear, especially closed-toe formal shoes for work, or tightly strapped sandals and juttis bought for a wedding months in advance, can become genuinely uncomfortable if your foot shape has shifted and you haven't accounted for it.** It's worth trying on wedding or festival footwear again closer to the actual event rather than relying on a fitting from months earlier, since swelling tends to be worse during long days of standing, heat, and salty festival food. If your work requires formal closed shoes, choosing a slightly roomier fit or a style with some give, rather than the snug fit you may have preferred in your twenties and thirties, reduces discomfort considerably. Mentioning persistent swelling to your gynaecologist is also worthwhile if it's frequent, since it can be one more data point in understanding your broader hormonal pattern alongside things like bloating or water retention elsewhere in the body.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Will my feet go back to their old size after menopause?**
Fluid-related daily swelling may improve somewhat once hormone levels stabilise post-menopause, but a structural change from ligament laxity or weight gain is less likely to fully reverse on its own.

**Is it normal for only one foot to feel more swollen than the other?**
Occasional minor asymmetry can happen, but persistent, noticeable one-sided swelling should be checked by a doctor rather than assumed to be hormonal, since it can have other causes.

**Should I buy a size up permanently?**
If you've noticed a consistent, lasting change rather than just daily fluctuation, getting properly re-measured and buying based on your current foot size, rather than clinging to your old size, is usually more comfortable and reduces the risk of foot problems from ill-fitting shoes.

**Can hormone therapy help with foot swelling?**
It may help with fluid retention generally for some women, as part of broader symptom management, though it isn't specifically prescribed for foot swelling alone. It's worth discussing with your doctor as part of your overall symptom picture.
