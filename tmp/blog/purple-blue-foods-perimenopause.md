Brinjal, jamun, black grapes, purple cabbage, deeply pigmented purple and blue foods owe their striking colour to anthocyanins, a group of antioxidant plant compounds studied for their role in brain health, blood vessel function, and reducing inflammation, all genuinely relevant during perimenopause. This is the final piece in our series on eating by colour, completing our guides to green leafy, white and aromatic, brown and whole, and red and orange foods.

## Why Purple and Blue Matters Now

**Anthocyanins and brain health.** As covered in our guide to brain fog in perimenopause, anthocyanins have been studied for supporting cognitive function and protecting brain cells from oxidative stress, a genuinely relevant, food-based support alongside sleep, exercise, and other measures.

**Supporting blood vessel health.** Anthocyanins support healthy blood vessel function, relevant to the circulation changes covered in our guides to cold hands and feet and varicose veins, and to the broader cardiovascular picture as heart risk rises with falling oestrogen.

**Reducing inflammation.** As covered in our guide to increased pain sensitivity in perimenopause, inflammation contributes to how much joint and muscle discomfort you experience. Anthocyanin-rich foods have anti-inflammatory properties that may offer some support alongside other measures.

**Fibre from brinjal and cabbage.** Purple cabbage and brinjal (eggplant) both offer fibre supporting the gut-hormone connection covered in our estrobolome guide, alongside their antioxidant content.

## The Foods Worth Prioritising

**Brinjal (baingan)** — versatile across Indian cooking, from baingan bharta to sabzi; the skin carries much of the beneficial pigment, so leaving it on where the recipe allows preserves more of the value.

**Jamun (Indian blackberry)** — a seasonal fruit particularly worth enjoying when available, traditionally associated with health benefits and rich in anthocyanins.

**Black grapes** — an easy, ready-to-eat snack, offering anthocyanins alongside hydration.

**Purple cabbage** — can substitute for regular cabbage in salads or light stir-fries, offering more antioxidant content for a similar dish.

**Beetroot** (also mentioned in our red and orange guide, genuinely spanning both categories) — its deep colour comes from a related but distinct pigment group, and it supports circulation and energy in its own right.

**Black or purple rice, where available** — increasingly accessible in India, offering more antioxidant content than polished white rice, alongside the whole-grain benefits covered in our brown and whole foods guide.

## How to Eat More Without It Feeling Like a Project

**Enjoy jamun and black grapes seasonally, without overthinking it.** These are simple, pleasant fruits to eat as they are, and enjoying them when in season is itself the habit worth building.

**Swap in brinjal regularly.** Rather than treating brinjal as an occasional dish, including it as a regular part of your vegetable rotation adds a genuinely valuable, differently pigmented food to your week.

**Try purple cabbage in place of regular cabbage** in a simple salad or slaw, an easy, low-effort swap with more nutritional value.

## A Word on Colour as a Simple Guide, Not a Rulebook

Across this four-part series, green, white, brown, and now red-orange and purple, the underlying principle has been the same: different plant pigments carry different beneficial compounds, and eating a genuinely varied, colourful diet, built from familiar, minimally processed Indian staples, does more for your body during perimenopause than any single "superfood." No one colour needs to dominate; the value comes from including all of them, regularly, across your week.

## When to See a Doctor

**Routine appointment** to discuss your overall diet as part of your broader perimenopause care, particularly if brain fog, joint discomfort, or circulation changes are significant features of your experience.

**Also worth checking** your inflammatory markers or relevant blood tests if joint pain or fatigue are persistent, since diet supports but does not replace a proper medical assessment of what is actually driving your symptoms.

Brinjal, jamun, black grapes, and purple cabbage round out a genuinely well-rounded, colour-based approach to eating through perimenopause. Together with the green, white, and red-orange foods covered in our earlier guides, they offer a simple, practical, and thoroughly Indian way to eat well through this transition.
