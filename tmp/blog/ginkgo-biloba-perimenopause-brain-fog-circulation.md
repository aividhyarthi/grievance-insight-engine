For perimenopausal women dealing with word-finding trouble, forgetting why they walked into a room, or feeling like their thinking has gone slightly foggy, ginkgo has become an increasingly common recommendation. Before adding it to an already long list of things you are trying for brain fog, it is worth understanding what this specific, extensively studied supplement actually does and does not do.

## What Is Ginkgo Biloba and Why Is It So Widely Studied?

**Ginkgo biloba is an extract derived from the leaves of one of the oldest living tree species, and it has been used in traditional Chinese medicine for centuries, primarily for respiratory and circulatory complaints.** Its modern popularity in the West and in India stems from decades of pharmaceutical-grade standardised extract research, which is part of why it has such an unusually large body of clinical trial data compared to most herbal supplements, most of it conducted on a specific standardised extract rather than ground leaf powder.

**The proposed mechanisms are reasonably well understood: ginkgo appears to improve peripheral blood flow and has antioxidant properties**, both of which plausibly connect to brain function given how dependent cognitive performance is on adequate cerebral blood flow and oxidative stress management.

## Does Ginkgo Actually Help With Brain Fog in Perimenopause?

**This is where the evidence gets noticeably thinner than ginkgo's broader reputation suggests.** Most of the large, well-designed trials on ginkgo and cognition have been conducted in older adults, often those with diagnosed mild cognitive impairment or early dementia, not in perimenopausal women experiencing hormonally driven brain fog. The underlying mechanism of cognitive change is genuinely different in these two groups: perimenopausal brain fog is primarily driven by fluctuating oestrogen's effects on specific brain regions involved in memory and verbal retrieval, whereas age-related decline involves broader, different neurological processes.

**There is currently no large, dedicated trial testing ginkgo specifically against perimenopausal cognitive symptoms**, which means any benefit for word-finding trouble, forgetfulness, or mental fatigue during this transition is extrapolated rather than directly proven. Some women report subjective improvement in focus and mental clarity, which is worth acknowledging, but this is different from established, reliable clinical evidence.

## What About the Bleeding Risk?

**This is the single most important safety consideration with ginkgo, and it deserves more attention than it usually gets in casual recommendations.** Ginkgo has documented antiplatelet effects, meaning it can reduce the blood's ability to clot, which becomes a genuine concern if combined with blood-thinning medication, regular aspirin, or ahead of any planned surgery, including minor dental procedures. Case reports exist of unusual bleeding events in people combining ginkgo with anticoagulant medication, and most surgeons and dentists now routinely ask patients to stop ginkgo at least a week or two before any procedure.

**If you are already on hormone therapy or any other regular medication, tell your doctor before adding ginkgo**, since interaction risk is not limited to blood thinners alone, and your doctor needs a complete picture of everything you are taking to interpret your symptoms and tests accurately.

## In the Indian Context

**Ginkgo biloba supplements are easily available in India, both as standalone capsules and within combination "memory support" or "brain health" blends, often alongside other ingredients like brahmi or omega-3s without clear labelling of each component's actual dose.** As with most supplements sold here, India's loosely regulated market means that the standardised extract used in most clinical trials may not match what is actually in a given commercial product, so a brand making strong cognitive claims is not automatically using the studied formulation or dose.

**If brain fog is significantly affecting your work or daily functioning, it is worth getting a baseline evaluation before trying supplements, since treatable contributors like thyroid dysfunction, iron deficiency, and poor sleep are common in this age group and often mistaken for "just" perimenopausal brain fog.** [Our free symptom check](/quiz) can help clarify how much of your cognitive symptoms are likely hormonal, and discussing both lifestyle strategies and, where appropriate, hormone therapy with a gynaecologist tends to address brain fog's underlying driver more directly than any single supplement.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is ginkgo biloba the same as brahmi or other Ayurvedic brain herbs?**
No. Ginkgo comes from traditional Chinese medicine and has a much larger body of standardised pharmaceutical-grade clinical research behind it, while brahmi and shankhpushpi are Ayurvedic herbs with a different traditional use history and a smaller, less standardised evidence base.

**How long does ginkgo take to show any effect, if it works at all?**
Most trials that found benefit used ginkgo consistently for several weeks to a few months before assessing outcomes; there is no reliable evidence of quick or dramatic improvement.

**Can I take ginkgo alongside hormone therapy?**
Possibly, but disclose it to your doctor first. The interaction concern is more about bleeding risk with other medications you may be on rather than a known direct interaction with hormone therapy itself.

**Who should avoid ginkgo entirely?**
Anyone on blood thinners or regular aspirin, anyone with a bleeding disorder, and anyone with a surgery or dental procedure scheduled in the near future should avoid it or discuss timing carefully with their doctor first.
