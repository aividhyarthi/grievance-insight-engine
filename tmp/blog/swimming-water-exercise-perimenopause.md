Our guide to the best exercise for perimenopause makes the case for strength training as the priority, and that still holds. But for many women, one particular form of exercise deserves its own spotlight: swimming and water-based workouts, which offer a distinct set of advantages precisely suited to several of the most common perimenopausal challenges.

## Why Water-Based Exercise Fits Perimenopause So Well

**It is genuinely joint-friendly.** As covered in our guide to joint pain and muscle aches in perimenopause, falling oestrogen makes joints more prone to stiffness and discomfort. Water supports body weight, dramatically reducing the impact on joints compared with running or many gym-based activities, making swimming an excellent option when joints are aching.

**It cools the body during a hot flash-prone stage.** Being in cool water is a direct, immediate way to manage the overheating so many women experience, offering comfort during exercise itself, rather than exercise being something to endure despite hot flashes.

**It supports heart health.** As covered in our guides to heart health and cholesterol changes in perimenopause, swimming is a genuinely effective form of cardiovascular exercise, engaging large muscle groups and elevating heart rate in a sustained, low-impact way.

**It offers real resistance for muscle.** Water provides natural resistance in every direction of movement, meaning swimming and water aerobics do contribute to muscle engagement, complementing, though not fully replacing, the dedicated strength training covered in our exercise guide.

**It can ease anxiety and support sleep.** Many women find the rhythmic, meditative quality of swimming genuinely calming, supporting the stress reduction covered in our anxiety and insomnia guides, and the physical exertion supports better sleep quality.

## Options Worth Considering

**Lap swimming.** A structured, effective full-body workout, adaptable to any fitness level by adjusting pace, distance, and stroke.

**Water aerobics or aqua fitness classes.** Often available at local pools or gyms, these offer structured, guided movement in water, well suited to those newer to exercise or seeking a social, class-based format.

**Water walking or jogging.** For those with significant joint pain or who are new to exercise, simply walking or gently jogging through waist-to-chest-deep water offers real cardiovascular benefit with minimal joint stress, an accessible starting point.

**Gentle floating and stretching.** Even simply moving gently and stretching in water, without a structured workout, offers relief for aching joints and a calming, low-pressure way to be active.

## Making It Practical

**Access matters, and options vary.** Availability of pools differs significantly across India by city and community, so this is a suggestion to weigh against what is realistically accessible to you, rather than an assumption that it is equally available to everyone.

**Combine, rather than replace.** As with our broader exercise guide, water-based activity works best as part of a varied routine, alongside dedicated strength training, rather than as a complete substitute for it.

**Start gently if new to exercise.** Even a short, comfortable session in the water a couple of times a week is a genuine, worthwhile start, with room to build gradually.

## When to See a Doctor

**Before starting**, if you have a heart condition, significant joint problems, or any other health concern, so a water-based programme can be tailored safely to you.

**Routine appointment** to discuss your overall exercise plan as part of your broader perimenopause care, particularly if joint pain or hot flashes have been limiting your ability to stay active in other ways.

If joint aches or hot flashes have made exercise feel like something to dread rather than something that helps, swimming and water-based workouts are genuinely worth considering, offering real cardiovascular and joint benefits in a form that works with your changing body, rather than against it.
