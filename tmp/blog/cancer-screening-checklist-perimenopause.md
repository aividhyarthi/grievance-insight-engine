Perimenopause brings a lot of new symptoms to think about, but it is also exactly the right moment to be proactive about something easy to postpone: routine health screening. Several important tests become more relevant, or newly relevant, in your 40s and 50s, and getting into a simple, regular rhythm with them is one of the most protective things you can do for your long-term health. Here is a clear, practical checklist.

## Why This Life Stage Matters for Screening

As oestrogen falls, your risk profile for several conditions genuinely shifts, bone density loss accelerates, heart disease risk rises toward that of men the same age, and breast and reproductive health need continued attention through and beyond this transition. Perimenopause is a natural, sensible point to build a screening routine, rather than something to address only if a problem appears.

## The Main Tests to Discuss With Your Doctor

**Mammogram.** Screening mammograms are generally recommended starting somewhere in your 40s, with frequency (annual or every two years) depending on your personal and family risk factors, guidelines vary, so this is a conversation to have directly with your doctor rather than following a one-size-fits-all rule. If you have a family history of breast cancer, this conversation is worth having proactively and may mean starting earlier or screening more frequently. Our guide to breast lumps and changes in perimenopause covers what to watch for between scans.

**Pap smear and HPV testing.** Cervical screening continues to matter through perimenopause and beyond, typically every three to five years depending on the specific test used and your history, and does not stop simply because periods become irregular or eventually cease. Ask your doctor for your personalised schedule.

**Bone density scan (DEXA).** As covered in our detailed guide to understanding your bone density results, this is worth discussing as a baseline test in perimenopause, particularly with risk factors like family history, early menopause, or a smaller body frame, since bone loss is silent until a scan or a fracture reveals it.

**Blood pressure.** Should be checked at least annually, more often if it has ever been raised, since blood pressure risk increases with age and hormonal change, and it usually causes no symptoms until it becomes a problem.

**Cholesterol (lipid profile).** As covered in our piece on cholesterol changes in perimenopause, this is worth checking as a baseline in your 40s if you have not had it done recently, since levels commonly shift with falling oestrogen even without other changes.

**Blood sugar (HbA1c or fasting glucose).** Diabetes risk rises with age, and perimenopause itself can affect blood sugar regulation. An annual check, especially with any family history, is a sensible, simple step.

**Thyroid function.** Thyroid problems become more common around this age and can closely mimic or overlap with perimenopause symptoms, as covered in our guide to telling perimenopause and thyroid issues apart. Worth checking if you have not recently, particularly with fatigue, weight change, or mood symptoms.

**Vitamin D and iron.** Not always automatically tested, but worth specifically requesting, given how common deficiency is in India and how much both affect energy, mood, bone health, and symptoms like restless legs.

**Eye examination.** As covered in our piece on vision changes in perimenopause, a routine eye check matters more at this age, both for comfort and because certain eye conditions become more common and are best caught early.

**Dental check-up.** Regular dental visits matter, partly because hormonal changes can affect gum health and oral tissue, as covered in our guide to oral symptoms in perimenopause.

**Skin check.** Given the significant time many Indian women spend in the sun over a lifetime, a periodic skin check for any new or changing moles or spots is a sensible, simple addition, particularly if you have not had one before.

## Making It Manageable

**Keep a simple record.** Note the date and result of each screening test, so you and any doctor you see know what is due and when, rather than relying on memory. This pairs well with the symptom diary covered in our related guide.

**Bundle where you can.** Many of these can be discussed and, where relevant, arranged at a single comprehensive check-up with your gynaecologist or general physician, rather than needing separate visits for everything.

**Do not let irregular periods delay screening.** It is a common misconception that pap smears or other screening should wait until periods settle. They should not; continue on your recommended schedule regardless of cycle changes.

## When to See a Doctor

**Routine appointment**, ideally once a year, specifically to review which of these screenings you are due for, given your age, family history, and personal risk factors, and to build a schedule you can actually stick to.

**Promptly**, outside of routine screening, for any new symptom that concerns you, a breast lump, unusual bleeding, persistent pain, or anything covered elsewhere in our symptom guides, rather than waiting for your next scheduled check-up.

Perimenopause is a natural moment to shift from reactive to proactive health care. A clear, personalised screening checklist, built with your doctor, is one of the simplest and most effective things you can do for the decades ahead.
