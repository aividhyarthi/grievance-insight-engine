Two new glasses prescriptions in two years, and headlights on the highway still look hazy, almost smeared, at night. Colours seem a little duller than they used to. It's tempting to write this off as ordinary ageing eyes, especially if everything else in midlife already feels like it's quietly shifting. But blurring vision that glasses no longer fully correct is one of the more recognisable early signs of a cataract, and it has nothing to do with testosterone.

This article explains what a cataract actually is, why some men develop them earlier than others, and what the surgery and its cost in India actually involve.

## What exactly is a cataract, and how is it different from just needing new glasses?

**The eye's natural lens is normally clear, focusing light onto the retina the way a camera lens focuses onto film.** A cataract is a gradual clouding of that lens, caused by changes in its proteins as the lens ages. This is fundamentally different from a simple refractive error, which is about the eye's shape and focusing power, correctable with a lens prescription. A cataract physically blocks and scatters light, so no glasses prescription, however precise, can fully correct the blur it causes.

**This is exactly why a man might notice his glasses "stop working" even after an update: the problem has shifted from focusing power, which glasses fix, to light actually getting through the lens clearly, which they can't.** Early cataracts often show up first as glare around headlights and streetlights at night, a general haziness, or colours looking slightly washed out or yellowed.

## Why do cataracts show up earlier for some men than others?

**Age is the single biggest factor, with cataracts becoming common from the 60s onward, but a meaningful number of men develop early cataracts in their 40s and 50s, particularly when other risk factors are present.** Diabetes is one of the strongest of these, both increasing cataract risk and tending to bring it on at a younger age than in non-diabetic men. Prolonged, unprotected sun exposure over decades, which is genuinely significant given how strong sunlight is across much of India, is another consistent risk factor, along with smoking and long-term use of steroid medication for other conditions like asthma or autoimmune disease.

### Does falling testosterone have anything to do with it?

**There is no established link between testosterone levels and cataract development.** Cataracts are not listed as a symptom of andropause or late-onset hypogonadism (LOH), and testosterone replacement therapy (TRT) has no known preventive or protective effect on the eye's lens. Where hormones genuinely matter for eye health in midlife is thyroid function and blood sugar control through diabetes, not testosterone specifically.

## What does cataract surgery actually involve, and what does it cost in India?

**Cataract surgery is a short, outpatient procedure in which the eye's clouded natural lens is removed and replaced with a clear artificial lens.** It is generally done one eye at a time, takes well under an hour per eye, and most men go home the same day with a short recovery period before vision clarity improves noticeably. It is genuinely one of the most common and successful surgical procedures in modern medicine, with high success rates for restoring clear vision.

**Cost in India varies widely depending on the hospital, city, and type of artificial lens chosen, ranging roughly from ₹20,000 to ₹60,000 or more per eye at private hospitals, while many government hospitals and dedicated eye-care camps offer the procedure at significantly lower cost or free for eligible patients.** Health insurance often covers at least a portion of the cost, so it's worth checking your policy specifically rather than assuming it isn't covered. There's no need to wait until vision is completely lost in an eye; the right timing for surgery is a conversation to have with your eye doctor once cataracts start meaningfully interfering with daily activities like driving or reading.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can cataracts come back after surgery?**
The artificial lens implanted during surgery cannot itself develop a cataract, so a true recurrence doesn't happen. Some patients develop clouding of the membrane behind the new lens months or years later, which is easily treated with a quick, painless outpatient laser procedure.

**Is cataract surgery painful?**
Most patients report only mild discomfort, not significant pain, since the eye is numbed beforehand. Any soreness afterward is usually mild and manageable with the eye drops your doctor prescribes for the recovery period.

**Should I wait until I'm completely unable to see out of that eye?**
No, there's generally no benefit to waiting that long, and it can make daily activities like driving unnecessarily unsafe in the meantime. Your eye doctor can help you decide on timing based on how much the cataract is actually affecting your daily life, not an arbitrary severity threshold.

**Can diet or supplements prevent cataracts?**
There's modest evidence that a diet rich in antioxidants, from fruits and vegetables, may offer some protective benefit, but no supplement has strong evidence for reversing or reliably preventing cataracts once the underlying ageing and risk factor process is underway. Wearing sunglasses that block UV light and managing diabetes well are the more solidly evidence-backed preventive steps.

A cataract is simply a part of the eye ageing, accelerated by a handful of well-understood risk factors that have nothing to do with testosterone. If new glasses aren't fixing the haze the way they used to, or night driving feels increasingly unsafe, that's worth a proper eye examination rather than another prescription update.
