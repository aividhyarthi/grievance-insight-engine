Saffron, known as kesar in most Indian households, shows up in wedding-season milk, festive sweets, and generations of advice from mothers and mothers-in-law about what a man should eat for "strength" and vitality. It is also one of the more expensive spices in the world, which makes the question of whether it actually does anything for libido or testosterone more than academic. Here is what the research says, separated from the kitchen folklore.

## What does saffron actually have good evidence for?

**Saffron's most consistently replicated evidence is for mood, not hormones.** Multiple small-to-moderate clinical trials, several of them well-designed and placebo-controlled, have found that saffron supplementation can meaningfully improve symptoms of mild to moderate depression, with some studies comparing its effect favourably to standard antidepressant medication over a short trial period. This is a genuinely notable body of research for a kitchen spice, and it is the strongest scientific ground saffron stands on.

This matters directly for andropause, because low mood, irritability, and loss of interest in things a man previously enjoyed are common symptoms of declining testosterone, and they are also symptoms of depression in its own right. Saffron's documented mood effect may offer real, independent benefit here, separate from whatever is happening with testosterone itself.

### What about sexual function specifically?

**A smaller set of trials has looked at saffron and sexual function, with some encouraging but preliminary results.** A few studies found that saffron supplementation improved erectile function and sexual satisfaction scores in men, including a notable trial in men experiencing sexual side effects from antidepressant medication, where saffron showed measurable improvement compared to placebo. This research is promising but still limited in scale, and it has not been consistently replicated across larger, more diverse populations, including Indian men specifically.

## Does saffron actually raise testosterone?

**The evidence here is much thinner than the mood and sexual-function research.** A few small animal studies have reported changes in testosterone-related markers after saffron extract, but these do not reliably translate into confirmed effects in human clinical trials. The handful of human studies that exist are small, short, and have not consistently shown a meaningful testosterone increase. This is an important distinction: saffron's apparent benefit for sexual satisfaction in the studies above may run through improved mood, reduced anxiety, and better blood flow, rather than through raised testosterone itself.

### Why does Indian tradition link saffron to male vitality?

Ayurvedic and traditional Indian food culture has long treated saffron as a warming, strengthening ingredient, often recommended in milk for energy, mood, and reproductive health in a broad, holistic sense rather than a specific hormonal claim. This traditional framing predates modern clinical research by centuries and should be understood on its own terms, as a cultural and culinary practice with plausible but not precisely mapped benefits, rather than read backwards as an ancient confirmation of a specific testosterone claim. A qualified Ayurvedic practitioner can offer personalised guidance on saffron's traditional use that goes beyond what a general wellness article can responsibly cover.

## What should a man actually check before buying saffron for this purpose?

**Saffron is one of the most commonly adulterated spices sold in India**, often diluted with dyed corn silk, safflower threads, or other fillers that look similar but carry none of saffron's active compounds. A man taking saffron specifically for a mood or libido effect, rather than just flavouring a dessert, should buy from a reputable, established seller and be sceptical of unusually cheap "pure kesar" claims, since genuine saffron is expensive to produce and priced accordingly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does saffron increase testosterone in men?**
The evidence for this specific claim is weak and mostly limited to animal studies. Saffron's better-supported effects are on mood and, in some smaller trials, sexual satisfaction, which may explain its traditional reputation without a direct testosterone mechanism.

**Can saffron help with low libido related to andropause?**
It may help indirectly, through its documented effects on mood and some evidence for improved sexual function, particularly in men with depression-related sexual side effects. It is not established as a direct treatment for low-testosterone-related libido changes.

**Is kitchen-variety saffron the same as what's used in studies?**
Not necessarily. Clinical trials typically use standardised saffron extract at a specific, measured dose, which is different from the small pinch added to milk or a sweet dish at home. The traditional culinary amount is unlikely to replicate a clinical dose.

**How can I tell if the saffron I'm buying is genuine?**
Genuine saffron is expensive, has a distinct bitter-sweet aroma, and typically bleeds a golden rather than deep red color when soaked briefly in warm water or milk. Buying from an established, reputable seller rather than an unusually cheap online listing reduces the risk of adulterated product.

Saffron's genuine evidence base for mood, and more cautiously for sexual satisfaction, makes it a reasonable, pleasant addition to a man's diet, consistent with centuries of Indian culinary tradition. What it is not, based on current research, is a confirmed testosterone booster, and a man dealing with persistent low libido or low mood deserves a proper medical evaluation rather than relying on a spice, however storied, to carry that weight alone.
