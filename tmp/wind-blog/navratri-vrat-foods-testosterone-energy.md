A man in his late forties observing a full nine-day Navratri fast for the first time in years notices that by day four he's running on singhara atta puris and fruit, and feeling noticeably more tired and foggy than he expected. He isn't eating less food than he thinks, he's eating the wrong combination of it, and that distinction matters more for a man already managing declining testosterone than it did when he fasted in his twenties.

This isn't about whether to fast, that's a personal and religious choice. It's about which vrat-permitted foods actually keep your energy and hormones steadier through the nine days, and which common choices quietly work against you.

## Why does Navratri fasting hit harder for men in midlife than it used to?

**A Navratri fast cuts out grains, lentils, and onion-garlic for nine days, which removes a lot of a typical Indian man's usual protein and fibre sources all at once.** If those aren't deliberately replaced with vrat-permitted alternatives, the fast quietly becomes a nine-day stretch of mostly starch and fried food, which is harder on blood sugar and energy than the pre-fast diet was. A man in his twenties with a higher baseline testosterone and more metabolic flexibility absorbs that shift more easily than a man in his forties or fifties already dealing with some degree of andropause.

### The problem usually isn't the fasting itself, it's the food choices within it

Sabudana vada, aloo chips, and sweetened makhana are technically vrat-permitted, but they are low in protein and high in refined starch or oil. Eating mostly these across nine days, meal after meal, produces the same blood sugar spikes and crashes that any fried, carb-heavy diet would, fasting or not.

## Which vrat foods actually help, and which ones work against you?

**Dairy is the single most useful protein source available during Navratri**, since paneer, dahi, and milk are all permitted and genuinely protein-dense. Nuts and seeds, particularly almonds and makhana roasted without excess ghee, add protein and healthy fat without breaking the fast's rules. Fruit provides fibre, potassium, and natural sugar that's gentler than refined starch, especially useful at a meal that would otherwise be entirely fried.

| Vrat food | Protein content | Best use |
|---|---|---|
| Paneer | High | Main protein at one or two meals a day |
| Dahi (curd) | Moderate-high | With fruit or as a side at every meal |
| Milk | Moderate | Through the day, helps steady hunger |
| Makhana (roasted, not fried in ghee) | Moderate | Snack, better than fried alternatives |
| Almonds and nuts | Moderate | Small handful between meals |
| Sabudana vada (fried) | Low | Occasional, not as a staple |
| Aloo chips/fries | Very low | Minimise, mostly starch and oil |
| Singhara or kuttu atta roti (unfried) | Moderate | Better than fried puris of the same flour |

## How should a man with declining testosterone actually plan the nine days?

**Spacing protein across the one or two meals you eat matters more than the total quantity.** A single meal built entirely around fried sabudana, with no dairy or nuts anywhere in the day, leaves very little protein to support muscle and steadier blood sugar. Splitting paneer or dahi into both the day's meals, rather than loading it all at once, tends to keep energy steadier through the afternoon and evening.

## Does this replace the general medical advice about fasting and testosterone?

No, and it shouldn't be read that way. The hormonal mechanics of why extended fasting raises cortisol and can temporarily dent testosterone, and when a man on testosterone replacement therapy or with diabetes needs a doctor's input before a long fast, are the same regardless of which festival he's observing. This piece is specifically about the food choices within a Navratri fast, since the vrat food list is distinct enough from Ramzan or Ekadashi that it deserves its own practical guide. If you have diabetes, are on hormone treatment, or have any chronic condition, talk to your doctor before the fast starts, not after you're already a few days in and struggling.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I build muscle or maintain it during a nine-day Navratri fast?**
Maintaining is realistic if you prioritise paneer, dahi, and nuts at every meal, though most men will struggle to actively build muscle during a grain-free, legume-free week. The goal during the fast is minimising loss, not gaining, and returning to a normal protein intake afterward.

**Is sabudana khichdi a good protein source during the fast?**
Not really, sabudana itself is almost pure starch with very little protein. Adding peanuts and potato to the khichdi, which is traditional in many households, improves it somewhat, but it still needs a side of dahi or paneer to be a genuinely balanced meal.

**I feel much more tired by day five or six than earlier days, is that normal?**
Cumulative fatigue through a multi-day fast is common, especially if protein intake has been low and meals have leaned heavily on fried snacks. Adding dairy and nuts to the remaining days, staying hydrated, and prioritising sleep usually helps noticeably, and the fatigue should resolve within a few days of resuming normal eating.

**Should men with low testosterone avoid Navratri fasting altogether?**
Not necessarily. Most healthy men can observe the fast safely with sensible food choices. The exception is men on testosterone replacement therapy, with diabetes, or with another chronic condition, who should discuss the specific fast with their doctor beforehand rather than assuming it's automatically fine.

Navratri fasting is a meaningful practice for a lot of men, and keeping it manageable isn't about doing it less devoutly, it's about choosing the protein-rich options the vrat tradition already includes, instead of defaulting to the fried snacks that happen to be the most convenient.
