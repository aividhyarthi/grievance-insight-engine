A 44 year old man gets his annual corporate health checkup, sees his sugar and cholesterol come back "borderline," and files the report away without really discussing what it means for the next ten years. Another man in his early fifties has never had a checkup at all beyond an occasional fever visit, and only sees a doctor when his wife insists after noticing he seems constantly tired and uninterested in things he used to enjoy. Neither situation is unusual in India, where preventive checkups are inconsistent and testosterone testing in particular is almost never included by default.

A proper midlife health screening schedule is not complicated, but it does need to be intentional, since most standard corporate packages are built around a narrow set of common conditions and will not flag hormonal decline unless a man asks for it specifically.

## Why do most Indian health checkups miss hormonal health entirely?

**Standard corporate wellness packages and hospital "executive checkup" bundles are designed around the most common and well-established risks: heart disease, diabetes, and a handful of cancers.** These are genuinely important, but the package design means testosterone, thyroid, and vitamin D are frequently left out unless a man's employer has chosen a more comprehensive tier or he requests additions himself. A checkup can come back entirely "normal" on its printed summary while never having looked at the hormone most likely to explain persistent fatigue or low libido.

### The tests that should be non-negotiable after 40

Blood pressure and fasting blood sugar or HbA1c catch two of the biggest silent risks in Indian men, given how common hypertension and type 2 diabetes are even in relatively fit, non-obese men. A lipid profile flags cardiovascular risk early enough to act on through diet, exercise, or medication. Kidney and liver function tests are usually included in standard packages and are worth reviewing rather than skimming past.

## What does a genuinely useful midlife screening schedule look like?

| Test or check | Recommended starting age | Frequency | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blood pressure | 30s onward | Annually, or more often if elevated | Hypertension is common and often symptomless |
| Fasting blood sugar or HbA1c | 35 to 40 | Annually | Type 2 diabetes and prediabetes are widespread and often silent |
| Lipid profile | 35 to 40 | Every 1 to 2 years | Cardiovascular risk assessment |
| Testosterone (total, and free if indicated) | On symptoms, or from mid-40s if requested | As advised by a doctor | Not routine, but relevant given how common andropause symptoms are |
| PSA and prostate discussion | Mid-40s, earlier with family history | As advised by a doctor | Prostate health screening |
| Eye and dental checks | 40s onward | Every 1 to 2 years | Often skipped despite being straightforward |

## How should a man actually raise the testosterone question with a doctor?

**Bringing a short, specific list of symptoms tends to work better than a vague complaint.** Rather than saying "I feel tired," it helps to say something closer to "I've been more tired than usual for a few months, my interest in intimacy has dropped, and I'm finding it harder to keep muscle even though I'm still going to the gym." That level of detail gives a doctor a clearer reason to add a testosterone test to the standard panel rather than treating fatigue as something to simply monitor.

## What if the corporate checkup package does not include what's needed?

Most employer-provided checkups can be supplemented with individual add-on tests at the same lab, usually at a modest additional cost, rather than requiring an entirely separate visit. It is reasonable to ask the checkup provider directly whether testosterone or a broader hormone panel can be added to an existing package.

### Cost is a real factor, and it is fine to be selective

Not every man needs every test every year. A doctor can help prioritise based on family history, symptoms, and prior results, so screening stays useful without becoming an expensive annual ritual done for its own sake.

## How does this fit into managing andropause specifically?

A testosterone result on its own is only one data point. A doctor will typically want to see it alongside symptoms, sometimes a repeat test to confirm a low reading, and other relevant results such as thyroid function or blood sugar, since several conditions can produce overlapping symptoms. This is exactly why a checkup that includes testosterone alongside the standard panel, rather than testosterone tested in isolation, tends to lead to a clearer picture.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**At what age should a man start getting a testosterone test as part of routine checkups?**
There is no fixed universal age, since testing is usually symptom-driven rather than routine. Many doctors will consider it from the mid-forties onward if fatigue, low libido, or other relevant symptoms are present, but it is reasonable to ask about it earlier if symptoms appear sooner.

**Do I need to fast before a testosterone blood test?**
Fasting requirements can vary by lab and by what else is being tested at the same time, so it is best to confirm directly with the lab or your doctor. Testosterone itself is typically drawn in the morning regardless, since levels are naturally highest then.

**Is a home testing kit for testosterone reliable?**
Home and at-home collection kits vary in quality and are not a substitute for a test ordered and interpreted by a doctor, particularly since a single result often needs clinical context and sometimes confirmation before any conclusion is drawn.

**How much does a comprehensive midlife checkup cost in India?**
Costs vary widely by city and provider, from a few thousand rupees for a basic package to considerably more for a comprehensive one including hormone panels. It is worth comparing what specific tests are included rather than choosing a package by price alone.

A midlife checkup is only as useful as the questions it is built to answer. Making sure testosterone is part of that conversation, when symptoms warrant it, turns a checkup from a formality into something that can actually explain what a man has been feeling.
