Almost everyone has had a foot or hand "fall asleep" after sitting awkwardly. That kind of brief, positional tingling is normal and harmless. But when numbness, tingling, or a burning or prickling sensation in the hands or feet starts showing up regularly after 40, without an obvious cause like sitting cross-legged too long, it deserves a closer look. It is one of the more common reasons people in their 40s, 50s, and 60s end up at a doctor's clinic, and in India specifically, it is very often connected to a condition many people do not realise they have.

This article covers what peripheral neuropathy actually is, the most common causes in Indian adults after 40, which symptoms genuinely need urgent attention, and what helps once a cause is identified.

## What is peripheral neuropathy, in plain terms?

**Peripheral neuropathy simply means damage or irritation to the nerves that carry sensation and movement signals between your brain, spinal cord, and the rest of your body, especially the hands and feet.** Because these nerves are long, with the ones running to the feet being the longest in the body, they are often affected first and most noticeably, typically starting as tingling, numbness, burning, or a "pins and needles" feeling that usually begins in the toes and sometimes moves upward over time if the underlying cause is not addressed.

## Why does this become more common after 40?

**The honest answer is that it is less about ageing itself and more about the medical conditions that become more common with age.** Blood vessels supplying the nerves can gradually stiffen, nerve conduction speed naturally slows a little with age, and crucially, conditions that directly damage nerves, chiefly diabetes, become far more prevalent in people's 40s, 50s, and beyond.

### Diabetes: the leading cause in India

India has one of the highest burdens of type 2 diabetes in the world, and a significant proportion of people living with it are undiagnosed. Chronically elevated blood sugar damages small nerve fibres over years, and diabetic peripheral neuropathy is frequently the first noticeable symptom that leads someone to get tested for diabetes in the first place, not the other way around. This is exactly why new numbness or tingling in the feet after 40 is a reasonable trigger for a blood sugar check, even if you have never been told you are diabetic or prediabetic.

### Other common causes worth knowing

**Vitamin B12 deficiency is common and frequently missed, particularly among vegetarians, strict vegans, older adults with reduced absorption, and anyone taking long-term acid-reducing medication or metformin.** Thyroid dysfunction, both underactive and overactive, can also cause nerve symptoms. Nerve compression at a specific point, such as carpal tunnel syndrome at the wrist, causes a more localised pattern, often in the thumb and first two fingers, worse at night. Heavy alcohol use over years, certain chemotherapy drugs, and kidney disease are other recognised causes.

| Possible cause | Typical pattern | What a doctor usually checks |
|---|---|---|
| Diabetes | Both feet, slowly progressive, often with burning at night | Fasting blood sugar, HbA1c |
| Vitamin B12 deficiency | Hands and feet, sometimes with fatigue or memory fog | Serum B12 level |
| Thyroid dysfunction | Variable, often with fatigue, weight or temperature changes | TSH and thyroid panel |
| Carpal tunnel syndrome | One hand, thumb and first two fingers, worse at night | Clinical exam, nerve conduction study if needed |
| Nerve compression in the back | One leg, may follow a specific line down the limb | Clinical exam, imaging if indicated |

**Sudden, one-sided numbness or weakness, especially if paired with slurred speech, facial drooping, or vision changes, can be a sign of stroke and needs emergency care immediately.** In India, call 112 or get to the nearest emergency department without delay in that scenario; do not wait to see if it passes. Gradual, symmetrical tingling in both feet over weeks or months is a very different and far more common picture, and while it still warrants a doctor's visit, it is not typically an emergency.

## What actually helps once a cause is found

**Treatment genuinely depends on the underlying cause, which is exactly why getting evaluated rather than self-treating with over-the-counter creams or home remedies matters.** Diabetic neuropathy is managed primarily through tighter blood sugar control, along with specific medications a doctor may prescribe for nerve pain. B12 deficiency is corrected with supplementation, usually resolving symptoms over weeks to months if caught reasonably early, though some nerve damage can be slow to reverse if deficiency has been long-standing. Carpal tunnel syndrome often responds to wrist splinting, activity changes, and sometimes a minor procedure. Foot care deserves special mention for anyone with diagnosed diabetes: numbness reduces your ability to feel small injuries, so a daily visual foot check, well-fitting footwear, and prompt attention to any cut or blister are genuinely protective habits, not optional extras.

A basic diagnostic workup (fasting blood sugar or HbA1c, vitamin B12, and a thyroid panel) is widely available and inexpensive at most diagnostic labs across India, often costing well under ₹2,000 for the full panel, which makes it a reasonable first step rather than something to postpone.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is occasional tingling in my hands or feet always a sign of a serious problem?**
No. Brief tingling after sitting in one position, sleeping on an arm awkwardly, or kneeling for a while is normal and resolves on its own within minutes. It becomes worth investigating when it happens repeatedly without an obvious positional cause, lasts longer, or is accompanied by weakness.

**Can peripheral neuropathy be reversed?**
It depends heavily on the cause and how early it is caught. Vitamin deficiency-related neuropathy often improves substantially once corrected. Diabetic neuropathy can often be slowed or stabilised with good blood sugar control, though some nerve damage that has already occurred may not fully reverse, which is why early detection matters.

**I'm a vegetarian with tingling in my feet. Should I just start taking a B12 supplement on my own?**
It is better to get a blood test first. Knowing your actual B12 level, and ruling out diabetes and thyroid issues at the same time, gives your doctor a much clearer picture than guessing, and the right dose of supplementation (if needed) depends on how deficient you actually are.

**Does this always mean I have diabetes?**
Not always, but it is common enough in Indian adults over 40 that most doctors will check blood sugar as a first step regardless of whether you have been diagnosed before. Many people discover prediabetes or diabetes this way.

Numbness and tingling are easy to brush off, especially when they come and go. But in your 40s and beyond, recurring symptoms in your hands or feet are worth a conversation with your doctor, not because it is always serious, but because the most common causes are genuinely manageable once correctly identified.
