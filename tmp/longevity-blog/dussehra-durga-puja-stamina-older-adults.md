Dussehra and Durga Puja ask more of the body than most festivals get credit for. There's the walking between pandals, the standing through hours of aarti, the sitting cross-legged on the ground at a Ramlila ground, and, if you're anywhere near a Durga Puja immersion procession, genuinely dense, slow-moving crowds that can stretch on for hours. None of this is a reason to sit the celebrations out. It is a reason to pace them a little differently than you might have in your 20s.

This isn't about doing less. It's about spreading the same enjoyment across the days in a way that doesn't leave you flattened by the time Vijayadashami actually arrives.

## What makes Dussehra and Durga Puja physically demanding for older adults?

**The combination of factors is what catches people off guard, rather than any single part of the festival being especially hard on its own.** An evening of pandal hopping often involves walking a few kilometres across uneven streets, standing in queues to view the idol, navigating steps or narrow lanes, and doing all of this in the lingering heat and humidity of early October, all while wearing festive clothes and footwear that may not be the most practical choice for walking.

**Durga Puja in particular adds an endurance element that other festivals don't quite match.** Pandal hopping across multiple neighbourhoods over several evenings, often in the evening crowds when everyone else is out too, means the fatigue is cumulative rather than contained to a single night.

## How much walking and standing is actually involved, and how do you pace it?

| Activity | Typical physical demand | How to pace it |
|---|---|---|
| Pandal hopping, one evening | 3-6 km of walking, often on uneven or crowded streets | Pick two or three pandals rather than trying to cover every one in a single evening |
| Standing through aarti | 20-40 minutes of standing in a dense crowd | Position yourself near a wall or pillar you can lean against |
| Ramlila ground, seated viewing | 1-2 hours sitting on the ground or a mat | Bring a small folding stool or thick cushion if floor-sitting is uncomfortable |
| Immersion (visarjan) procession | Hours of slow-moving, dense crowd exposure | Choose a viewing spot away from the main procession route, or watch a later, quieter stage of immersion |

## Managing the crowds: what about immersion processions and crushes?

**Durga Puja immersion processions are among the densest crowd situations in the Indian festival calendar, and the slow pace of a procession means you can be standing in a packed crowd for far longer than you'd expect.** For anyone with a heart condition, breathing difficulty, or balance concerns, watching the immersion from a slight distance, or choosing a quieter ghat and a later time slot once the initial rush has passed, is a reasonable and genuinely sensible adjustment, not something to feel you're missing out on.

**Footwear matters more than it gets credit for.** Festive footwear, especially heeled sandals, is a common cause of twisted ankles and blisters during long evenings of standing and walking on uneven pandal routes. A comfortable, closed, flat shoe that still looks presentable is worth prioritising over a purely decorative choice.

## What about joint pain from floor-sitting during aarti and Ramlila?

**Sitting cross-legged on the ground for an extended Ramlila performance or a long aarti can genuinely strain knees and hips that have grown less flexible over the years**, and getting up afterward after sitting still for over an hour is often the part people underestimate. Bringing a thin folding stool, a thick cushion, or simply choosing to stand toward the back where you can shift position periodically are all reasonable adaptations that don't take anything away from your participation. If getting up from the floor has become genuinely difficult, it's worth mentioning to your doctor, since it can sometimes point to a joint or mobility issue worth addressing on its own.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is it safe to fast and also walk long distances during Dussehra and Durga Puja festivities?**
Fasting combined with long hours of standing and walking in heat increases the risk of dehydration and low blood sugar, so if you're fasting on any of these days, pace your walking more conservatively, carry water, and break your fast with something light before heavy activity rather than after. Anyone managing diabetes or blood pressure should check with their doctor before combining fasting with an especially demanding festival schedule.

**What's the safest way to watch a Durga Puja immersion if I have mobility concerns?**
Choose a viewing point away from the densest part of the procession route, arrive early to secure a stable spot rather than trying to move through a forming crowd, and consider watching a later stage of immersion once the initial crush has thinned out. Going with a family member who can help you navigate the crowd is also a reasonable, sensible choice.

**How do I avoid feeling completely drained by the end of the festival?**
Spacing your pandal visits across the full nine or ten days, rather than trying to see everything in the first few evenings, is the single biggest factor. Building in at least one genuinely quiet evening partway through also helps prevent the cumulative fatigue that tends to catch up by Vijayadashami.

**Are there specific foods during this festival period that help with energy and stamina?**
A light, balanced meal before heading out, rather than a heavy one, tends to help with both energy and comfort while walking. Keeping sugary festive sweets to smaller portions, especially if you're also managing blood sugar, helps avoid the energy crash that can follow a very sugar-heavy prasad or snack.

None of this is about doing less of the festival. It's about spreading the same joy across more evenings, choosing your moments in a crowd carefully, and giving your body the breaks it's asking for, so you can genuinely enjoy Dussehra and Durga Puja rather than just get through them.
