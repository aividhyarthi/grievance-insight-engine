A fall, a stroke, a slow decline after a surgery: most Indian families end up making long-term care decisions in the middle of a crisis, with limited time, limited information, and a lot of guilt mixed in. Yet the actual options for care after 60, from a part-time home attendant to a residential care facility, have expanded considerably in Indian cities over the past decade, and understanding them ahead of time changes a stressful scramble into a manageable decision.

This piece lays out the realistic choices available for long-term care in India, roughly what they cost, and how to start the conversation with your family before an emergency forces it.

## Why do so few families plan for this ahead of time?

Talking about needing care one day touches on mortality, independence, and family duty all at once, which makes it an easy conversation to keep postponing. **Many adult children also assume, without ever directly asking, that a parent would always prefer to be cared for at home by family, when the parent's actual preference, and the family's actual capacity to provide that care long-term, may be quite different.** Add in the discomfort of discussing money, and it is easy to see why so many of these decisions get made for the first time during a hospital discharge conversation, with no prior groundwork at all.

This connects closely to the broader topic of legal and financial documentation after 50, since a will or power of attorney without an accompanying care plan only addresses part of what a family will actually need to navigate.

## What are the actual care options available after 60?

### Part-time or live-in home care

A home-care attendant, sometimes called an ayah or caregiver depending on the region and agency, can be hired for a few hours a day or around the clock, through dedicated home-care agencies that have grown substantially in most metro cities over the past several years. **This option allows a person to remain in familiar surroundings, which matters enormously for comfort and orientation, especially for someone managing early cognitive decline, but it does require either a family member coordinating the arrangement or an agency with reliable, trained, and background-verified staff.**

### Nursing care for medical needs

For situations involving wound care, injections, physiotherapy follow-up, or monitoring after a hospital stay, trained nursing attendants (rather than general home-care attendants) can be arranged through hospitals or specialised home healthcare agencies, usually at a higher cost than general home care.

### Assisted living and senior living communities

A newer but rapidly growing option in Indian cities, assisted living facilities offer private or shared accommodation along with meals, housekeeping, and varying levels of medical and personal care support on site. **These range considerably in what they offer, from largely independent senior communities with on-call medical support to facilities built specifically for higher-dependency care, so visiting in person and asking detailed questions about staff ratios and emergency response matters more than going by marketing material alone.**

### Residential nursing homes for higher-dependency care

For a person who needs round-the-clock medical supervision that is difficult to replicate at home, a residential nursing facility remains an option, though availability and quality vary considerably by city, and this is generally considered a later-stage option rather than a first step.

## What does this actually cost?

Costs vary enormously by city, by the specific agency or facility, and by the level of care needed, so treat the ranges below as a rough starting point for your own research, not a firm quote.

| Care option | Rough monthly cost range in a major Indian metro |
|---|---|
| Part-time home attendant (a few hours a day) | Roughly ₹8,000 to ₹20,000, depending on hours and city |
| Full-time live-in home attendant | Roughly ₹18,000 to ₹35,000, higher for trained nursing attendants |
| Assisted living, shared or independent unit | Roughly ₹30,000 to ₹80,000, depending on the facility and level of service |
| Assisted living with higher-dependency medical care | Often ₹60,000 and above, varying widely by facility and city |

**These figures can differ substantially in smaller towns, where costs are often lower but the number of trained agencies and facilities to choose from is also smaller.** Always get a written, itemised quote and ask specifically what is and is not included, since add-on charges for medical supplies, transport, or additional hours are common.

## Does health insurance cover any of this?

This is a common and important misunderstanding. **Most standard health insurance policies in India, including senior citizen health plans, are designed to cover hospitalisation and acute medical treatment, not ongoing custodial or long-term care such as a home attendant's salary or assisted-living fees.** A small number of specialised long-term care insurance products exist in the Indian market, but they remain far less common and less comprehensive than in some other countries, so most families are effectively self-funding long-term care costs through savings, family contribution, or, in some cases, reverse mortgage or property-related arrangements. This makes it worth reviewing your overall financial and insurance paperwork well before a care need becomes urgent.

## How do you actually start the conversation with your family?

**Start the conversation while the person who may need future care can still fully participate in the decision, ideally well before 70, rather than after a health scare makes it urgent.** Framing it as "let's figure out what you'd actually want" rather than "let's decide what we'll do to you" tends to go considerably better and preserves a sense of agency that matters enormously to most older adults. It also helps to involve all adult children in the same conversation at the same time, rather than relaying decisions secondhand, since long-term care planning is one of the more common sources of sibling conflict in Indian families when it is handled piecemeal.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**At what age should a family start planning for long-term care?**
There is no fixed age, but starting the conversation in the early-to-mid 60s, while the person involved is healthy and can participate fully in the planning, generally leads to better outcomes than waiting for a crisis.

**Is assisted living only for people without family support?**
No. Many families choose assisted living specifically because it offers structured support, social company, and professional care that complements rather than replaces family involvement, not because family support is absent.

**How do I check if a home-care agency is reliable?**
Ask for the agency's registration details, request references from current clients, confirm that staff undergo police background verification, and clarify training specifics for any medical tasks the attendant will perform.

**Can I rely on my children to provide full-time care instead of hiring help?**
Many families do combine family involvement with some paid support, but relying entirely on unpaid family caregiving, especially for a working adult child, often leads to significant burnout over time; planning a hybrid approach tends to be more sustainable.

Long-term care is one of those topics that feels easier to postpone than almost any other, right up until it is not. A calm conversation and some basic research now will serve your family far better than the same decisions made under pressure later.
