Earwax is one of those things nobody thinks about until it becomes a problem, usually announced by a muffled feeling in one ear, a day of sounds seeming oddly distant, or an unexpected itch that nothing seems to fix. After 40, this becomes a noticeably more frequent visitor for many people, and the common instinct to go in with a cotton swab usually makes things worse rather than better.

This article looks at why earwax buildup becomes more common with age, why so many popular home remedies for it are actually counterproductive, and what genuinely helps.

## Why does earwax buildup become more common after 40?

**Earwax, medically called cerumen, is produced continuously by glands in the outer ear canal and normally moves outward on its own**, carrying away dust, dead skin cells, and debris as part of the ear's natural self-cleaning process. This quiet, efficient system works well for most of early life without any help at all.

**As you age, earwax tends to become drier and the natural outward migration process slows down**, meaning it is more likely to accumulate rather than clear on its own. Ear canal hair, which helps trap debris, can also change with age in ways that make natural clearing less efficient. None of this is unusual or a sign that something is wrong; it is simply a normal shift that means some people need a little occasional help after 40 where they never did before.

### What makes it worse, even when well-intentioned?

**The single biggest contributor to worsened earwax buildup is cleaning habits meant to prevent it.** Cotton swabs, hairpins, rolled tissue corners, and similar objects inserted into the ear canal typically push existing wax further inward rather than removing it, compacting it against the eardrum where it becomes genuinely harder to clear and more likely to cause symptoms.

- **Cotton swabs** are the most common culprit, frequently cited by doctors as a leading cause of impacted earwax and occasional eardrum injury
- **Ear candles**, a popular alternative remedy, have no good evidence of effectiveness and carry a real risk of burns or wax dripping into the canal
- **Regular hearing aid or earphone use** can trap earwax against the canal wall and interfere with its natural outward movement over time
- **Very narrow or unusually shaped ear canals**, which some people simply have, make natural wax clearance less efficient regardless of cleaning habits

## What actually helps, safely?

**For most people with mild buildup and no pain, a few drops of a proper softening solution, such as plain warmed olive oil or a pharmacy-bought ear-softening drop, used as directed, is a reasonable first step.** This softens hardened wax and can allow it to clear on its own over a few days, though it does not work for everyone and should not be used if there is any ear pain, discharge, or suspicion of a perforated eardrum.

**For anything beyond mild, occasional buildup, proper removal by a doctor is the safest route.** Doctors use specific tools, microscopic visualisation, or gentle irrigation under controlled conditions, which is a fundamentally different and safer process than anything attempted at home with household objects.

## When does earwax buildup need medical attention?

**Most earwax buildup is harmless and resolves with simple softening or a doctor's gentle removal, but a few signs genuinely deserve prompt attention rather than a wait-and-see approach.**

| Sign | What it may indicate | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Gradual muffled hearing in one or both ears | Likely simple wax buildup | See a doctor for assessment and removal if needed |
| Sudden hearing loss | Could be wax, but also other causes needing urgent evaluation | See a doctor promptly, same day if possible |
| Ear pain or a feeling of fullness with fever | Possible infection, not just wax | See a doctor without delay |
| Discharge, blood, or foul smell from the ear | Possible infection or eardrum issue | See a doctor promptly, do not attempt home removal |
| Ringing in the ear alongside muffled hearing | Sometimes linked to wax, worth checking before assuming it is permanent tinnitus | A doctor can confirm whether wax is contributing |

**If you use hearing aids, it is worth asking your audiologist how often your specific device and ear canal shape require a wax check**, since this varies by individual and device type, and routine professional cleaning is a normal part of hearing aid maintenance rather than a sign that something has gone wrong.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is it normal to have more earwax than I used to?**
It can be, particularly if you use earphones, hearing aids, or have simply noticed your ears are drier in general as part of ageing skin changes elsewhere on your body. It is still worth having it checked if it is causing muffled hearing or discomfort, rather than assuming it will clear on its own indefinitely.

**Are ear cleaning kits sold at pharmacies safe to use at home?**
Simple softening drops are generally fine to use as directed. Bulb syringes for home irrigation carry some risk if used with too much force or if there is an underlying eardrum issue you are unaware of, so it is reasonable to ask a pharmacist or doctor before trying one, especially for a first attempt.

**Why do I feel worse after trying to clean my ears myself?**
This is extremely common and usually means wax has been pushed deeper rather than removed, which can temporarily worsen muffled hearing or create a feeling of fullness. It is a good reason to stop further home attempts and have a doctor assess and clear it properly.

**Can earwax buildup cause tinnitus?**
It can contribute to a temporary sensation of ringing or fullness in some people, and clearing the buildup sometimes resolves it. However, persistent tinnitus unrelated to wax has other causes entirely, so it is worth having it checked rather than assuming earwax is always the explanation.

Earwax buildup is one of those small, unglamorous changes that becomes more common after 40, and the good news is that the fix is usually simple once you stop trying to manage it with whatever is nearest in the bathroom cabinet. Leave ears mostly alone, use a proper softening drop for mild buildup, and let a doctor handle anything beyond that.
