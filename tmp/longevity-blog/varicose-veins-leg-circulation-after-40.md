If you have noticed your ankles swelling after a long day of standing, a heavy, tired feeling in your calves by evening, or faint bluish veins becoming visible on your legs, you are describing one of the most common, and most commonly ignored, changes of midlife. Varicose veins and circulation changes in the legs become noticeably more frequent after 40, yet many people dismiss them as a cosmetic issue or simply "getting older" rather than something worth a doctor's attention.

This article explains what is actually happening in your leg veins as you age, who is more at risk, and what genuinely helps, from simple daily habits to knowing when it is time to see a doctor.

## Why do veins in the legs start causing trouble after 40?

**Your leg veins contain small one-way valves whose job is to stop blood from flowing backward as it travels up against gravity toward the heart.** With age, the vein walls lose some elasticity and these valves can weaken, allowing a small amount of blood to flow backward and pool in the lower leg instead of moving efficiently upward. Over time, this pooling stretches the vein, and the vein becomes visibly enlarged, twisted, or bulging under the skin, which is what you see as a varicose vein.

This process is gradual and strongly influenced by a few specific factors beyond age alone:

- **Genetics**: A family history of varicose veins is one of the strongest predictors, so if your parents had them, your own risk is meaningfully higher
- **Prolonged standing or sitting**: Jobs that involve hours of standing (common among teachers, retail staff, and healthcare workers in India) or hours of sitting without movement both increase pressure in leg veins
- **Pregnancy history**: Increased blood volume and hormonal changes during pregnancy raise the risk of varicose veins, and the effect can be cumulative across multiple pregnancies
- **Body weight**: Extra body weight increases pressure on leg veins, compounding the effect of weakened valves
- **Prolonged heat exposure**: India's climate, with long hot seasons, can worsen the sensation of heavy, swollen legs, since heat causes veins to dilate further

## What does it actually feel like, and when is it more than cosmetic?

**Many people only think of varicose veins as the visible, rope-like veins on the surface of the leg, but the symptoms often appear before the veins become obviously visible.** Common early signs include a heavy or achy feeling in the legs by evening, mild ankle swelling that improves overnight, and visible small clusters of thin, reddish or bluish veins (spider veins) near the ankles or behind the knees.

### Signs that need a doctor's evaluation, not just home care

| Symptom | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Sudden swelling in one leg, not both | Can signal a blood clot (deep vein thrombosis) and needs urgent medical attention |
| Skin discolouration or hardening near the ankle | May indicate chronic venous insufficiency, which benefits from earlier treatment |
| A sore or ulcer near the ankle that is not healing | A sign of advanced venous disease that needs active medical management |
| Significant pain, warmth, or redness in the leg | Should be checked promptly, especially if it appears suddenly |
| Veins that bleed easily or are very tender to touch | Worth a vascular specialist's opinion |

**If you notice sudden, painful, one-sided leg swelling, this needs prompt medical attention rather than waiting**, since it can occasionally signal a blood clot, a genuine medical emergency. If you are unsure how urgent your symptoms are, call 112, India's emergency number, or get to a hospital promptly rather than waiting it out.

## What can you actually do about it day to day?

**For most people with mild to moderate symptoms, a combination of simple daily habits makes a genuine difference**, even without needing any procedure:

- **Keep moving**: If your job involves long hours of standing, try to walk a short distance every 30 to 45 minutes. If you sit for long stretches, such as during a desk job or a long commute, flex your ankles and calves periodically to help the leg muscles pump blood upward.
- **Elevate your legs**: Resting with your legs raised above heart level for short periods, particularly in the evening, can reduce the pooling effect and ease swelling.
- **Compression stockings**: Graduated compression stockings apply gentle pressure that helps veins work more efficiently. They are widely available in India through pharmacies, though getting properly fitted, ideally with a doctor's or pharmacist's guidance, matters more than choosing the cheapest option.
- **Maintain a healthy body weight**: Reducing excess pressure on leg veins through gradual, sustainable weight management can ease symptoms over time.
- **Stay physically active overall**: Regular walking and movement supports healthy circulation generally, and is covered in more depth in our piece on walking and longevity.

## Do home remedies and massage actually work?

**Gentle leg massage and traditional practices like foot care routines can feel genuinely soothing and may offer temporary symptom relief**, but they do not reverse the underlying weakened valves causing varicose veins. Be cautious about any product claiming to "cure" or "dissolve" varicose veins without a medical procedure behind it, since no cream or oil can repair a damaged vein valve. For more significant or worsening varicose veins, a vascular doctor can discuss options ranging from continued conservative management to minimally invasive procedures, depending on severity.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Are varicose veins just a cosmetic issue?**
Not always. While many cases are primarily a cosmetic concern, varicose veins can progress to cause real discomfort, swelling, skin changes, and in more advanced cases, ulcers. It is worth getting assessed rather than assuming it is purely appearance-related.

**Can exercise make varicose veins worse?**
Generally no. Regular, moderate exercise like walking actually supports healthy circulation by helping the calf muscles pump blood back toward the heart. Very heavy, repetitive straining exercises without adequate rest might aggravate symptoms in some people, but routine movement is beneficial, not harmful.

**Do compression stockings need to be worn all day?**
This depends on your specific situation and symptom severity, and is best decided with a doctor's guidance. Many people wear them during the hours they are most active or standing, and remove them at night.

**Is there a genetic test to predict my risk?**
No specific genetic test is used in routine practice. The most practical way to assess your risk is simply knowing your family history and discussing it with your doctor if you notice early symptoms.

Leg circulation changes after 40 are common, usually manageable, and rarely an emergency on their own, but they are also easy to dismiss for far too long. A little daily attention, and a doctor's opinion when something changes suddenly, go a long way toward keeping your legs comfortable for decades to come.
