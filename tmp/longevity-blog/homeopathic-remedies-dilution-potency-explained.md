Walk into most Indian homes over 40 and there is a decent chance a small box of white pellets or a dropper bottle sits in a drawer somewhere, kept for coughs, colds, or a child's fever. Yet very few people who reach for these remedies, including many who use them regularly, can actually explain how they are made. The process, known as potentisation, is more unusual than most people realise, and understanding it is genuinely useful for deciding how much weight to give the remedy and why scientists remain so divided on the subject.

This piece walks through what dilution and potency actually mean in homeopathy, why the numbers involved are so large, and what that implies for how these remedies might, or might not, work.

## What does "potency" actually mean in homeopathy?

In conventional medicine, a higher dose usually means more active ingredient and often a stronger effect. Homeopathy works on the opposite logic. **A higher potency, such as 200C, means the original substance has been diluted more times, not that more of it is present.** The number before the letter tells you how many rounds of dilution happened, and the letter tells you the dilution ratio used at each round.

The two most common scales are C (centesimal, a 1-in-100 dilution each round) and X (decimal, a 1-in-10 dilution each round). So a "30C" remedy has gone through 30 separate rounds of 1-in-100 dilution, with the mixture shaken firmly against a hard surface, called succussion, after each round. This shaking step is considered central to the process in homeopathic theory, distinguishing potentisation from simply watering something down.

### Why the maths gets extreme quickly

Each round of 1-in-100 dilution multiplies the previous dilution by another hundred. By the time you reach 6C, the original substance has been diluted by a factor of 10 to the power of 12. By 30C, a commonly sold potency, the dilution factor is roughly 10 to the power of 60. For comparison, Avogadro's number, which describes roughly how many molecules exist in a standard chemistry sample, is about 6 times 10 to the power of 23. Past a certain dilution point (generally agreed to be somewhere around 12C), the odds that even a single molecule of the original substance remains in the final preparation become vanishingly small.

## If nothing measurable remains, how do practitioners say it works?

This is the crux of the scientific disagreement, and it is worth understanding both sides honestly rather than dismissing one of them outright.

**The homeopathic explanation rests on a concept sometimes called "water memory" or an energetic imprint.** Classical homeopathic theory holds that the succussion process imprints some kind of pattern or "memory" of the original substance onto the water or alcohol molecules, and that this pattern, not the original chemical, is what produces the therapeutic effect. This idea was proposed in the late eighteenth century by Samuel Hahnemann, long before modern chemistry and molecular biology existed to test it.

**Mainstream chemistry and physics have not found a verified mechanism that supports this claim.** Water molecules are in constant, rapid motion and do not maintain a stable structural "memory" of a substance for any meaningful length of time at room temperature, according to current physical chemistry. Multiple attempts to detect a measurable physical difference between a homeopathic ultra-dilution and plain diluent, using rigorous, blinded laboratory methods, have not produced a consistent, independently replicated result. This is a major reason why most pharmacology and chemistry researchers remain sceptical of the mechanism, independent of how any individual patient feels after taking a remedy.

## Does this mean the remedies cannot possibly have any effect?

Not entirely, and it is worth being precise here rather than overstating either position.

| What might explain a benefit | How strong is the evidence |
|---|---|
| The placebo effect and expectation | Well documented; can meaningfully affect how symptoms are perceived, especially for pain, fatigue, and mood |
| The ritual and attention of a consultation | Genuinely helps some people feel cared for and heard, independent of the remedy itself |
| Natural recovery over time | Many minor ailments (colds, mild aches) improve on their own regardless of what is taken |
| A direct pharmacological effect of the ultra-diluted substance itself | Not supported by a verified mechanism or consistent trial evidence at this time |

**None of this means a person's experience of feeling better is fake or imagined.** Feeling cared for, having a ritual to follow, and believing a remedy will help are all genuinely powerful and legitimate contributors to how people experience symptoms. What it does mean is that the specific chemical content of an ultra-diluted remedy is unlikely to be doing the heavy lifting, which matters most for anything beyond mild, self-limiting symptoms.

## Does potency matter for safety?

For most ultra-diluted preparations (12C and above), the near-total absence of the original substance generally means a very low risk of direct chemical toxicity or drug interaction from the remedy itself. **This is different from low-potency preparations (such as mother tinctures or very low X or C potencies), which may still contain a meaningful amount of the original substance and should be treated with the same caution as any active ingredient.** Always tell your doctor about every remedy you are taking, including homeopathic ones, especially if you are managing a chronic condition or taking regular prescription medicines, since even a mislabelled or adulterated product can occasionally pose a real risk.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is a higher potency like 200C "stronger" than a lower one like 6C?**
Not in the conventional pharmacological sense. A higher potency number means more dilution steps were performed, not a higher concentration of active substance. Homeopathic practitioners use potency selection based on their own clinical framework, which is distinct from how dose-response works in conventional pharmacology.

**Do homeopathic remedies actually contain any of the original substance?**
At low potencies, possibly small chemical traces. At commonly sold higher potencies such as 30C or 200C, standard chemistry suggests essentially none of the original substance is likely to remain in a typical dose.

**Why do some people swear these remedies work for them?**
The placebo effect, the attentive ritual of a consultation, and the fact that many minor symptoms resolve on their own over time are all genuine, well-documented reasons someone might feel better after taking a remedy, independent of its chemical content.

**Should I stop taking a homeopathic remedy if this explanation makes me sceptical?**
That is a personal decision, but what should not change is keeping your doctor informed about everything you take and never substituting a homeopathic remedy for medically necessary diagnosis or treatment of a specific condition.

Understanding how a remedy is actually prepared does not have to be dismissive of the people who use it or the comfort it can bring. It simply gives you a clearer, more honest basis for deciding what role, if any, it should play alongside the medical care that matters most for your long-term health.
