India is home to the largest number of registered homeopathic practitioners anywhere in the world, with several hundred thousand doctors holding formal homeopathic qualifications. For many Indian families, a homeopathic doctor, addressed with the same "Dr." title as a physician trained in modern medicine, is a familiar and trusted part of healthcare, often consulted for chronic, minor, or ageing-related concerns. But what does that formal training actually involve, how is the field regulated, and what should you know before choosing a practitioner? This is worth understanding clearly, separate from any judgement on homeopathy's effectiveness, which we have covered elsewhere.

## What formal training does a homeopathic doctor in India actually complete?

**The standard qualification is BHMS, Bachelor of Homoeopathic Medicine and Surgery, a government-recognised undergraduate degree that takes about five and a half years, including a compulsory year of internship.** This is a genuinely rigorous academic path, not a short certificate course. The curriculum includes core modern medical sciences, anatomy, physiology, pathology, and pharmacology among them, alongside homeopathic materia medica, repertory (the systematic method homeopaths use to match symptoms to remedies), and homeopathic philosophy.

Some practitioners go further with postgraduate degrees (MD in Homoeopathy) in specific specialisations. It is worth knowing that this training is distinct from, and shorter than, the MBBS degree (five and a half years as well, but with a very different curriculum weighted heavily toward diagnosing and treating disease using modern medical methods, plus further years of specialisation for most fields).

## How is homeopathy actually regulated in India?

**Homeopathic education and practice in India is regulated by the National Commission for Homoeopathy (NCH), established in 2020, which replaced the earlier Central Council of Homoeopathy.** The NCH sets curriculum standards, accredits homeopathic medical colleges, and maintains a central register of qualified practitioners. At the state level, State Homoeopathy Councils handle registration and licensing within each state.

This is a genuinely separate regulatory structure from the National Medical Commission, which oversees MBBS education and modern medical practice. The two systems run in parallel, which is part of why it matters to know which kind of "doctor" you are consulting, and for what purpose.

## What can a qualified homeopathic doctor's scope of practice actually cover?

**This is a genuinely contested and evolving area, and it varies somewhat by state.** In principle, BHMS training includes enough modern medical science that some Indian states have, controversially, permitted qualified homeopathic doctors to prescribe a limited list of modern allopathic medicines after additional bridge courses, a policy that has drawn criticism from modern medical bodies who argue the training is insufficient for that scope. This remains a genuinely unresolved and debated policy question in Indian healthcare, not a settled matter.

What is much less contested is that homeopathic doctors are trained specifically in homeopathic diagnosis and remedy selection, and that for diagnosing new or serious symptoms, managing chronic diseases like diabetes or heart disease, prescribing medication for such conditions, or handling any medical emergency, an MBBS-trained doctor with the appropriate specialisation is essential. Most homeopathic doctors themselves would agree with this distinction, and responsible practice on both sides involves recognising when to refer a patient onward.

## Why does this distinction matter more after 40?

As health needs after 40 increasingly involve chronic disease management, multiple medications, and higher stakes if a serious diagnosis is missed, understanding exactly what kind of medical training a practitioner has becomes more important, not less. This is not a judgement on homeopathy's value for the concerns people genuinely use it for, general wellbeing, minor and self-limiting complaints, or as a complementary approach alongside standard medical care. It is simply about making informed decisions about who to consult for what, and ensuring nothing serious goes undiagnosed while pursuing any complementary approach.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is BHMS a recognised medical degree in India?**
Yes, BHMS is a government-recognised degree awarded by accredited institutions and regulated by the National Commission for Homoeopathy. It is a genuine, multi-year academic qualification, distinct from MBBS, with its own regulatory and registration system.

**Can a homeopathic doctor legally prescribe modern medicines in India?**
This varies by state and remains a debated policy area. Some states permit it under specific conditions or after additional bridge courses, while modern medical bodies have raised concerns about adequacy of training for that scope. It is reasonable to ask a practitioner directly about their specific qualifications and what they are permitted to prescribe in your state.

**How can I verify if a homeopathic doctor is actually registered?**
Most State Homoeopathy Councils maintain a register of qualified, licensed practitioners, and many now have searchable online registers. Asking a practitioner directly for their registration number and BHMS qualification details is a reasonable and normal question to ask.

**Should I see a homeopathic doctor or an MBBS doctor for a new health concern after 40?**
For any new, persistent, or concerning symptom, especially anything that could indicate a serious condition, an MBBS doctor should be the first point of contact for proper diagnosis. A qualified homeopathic doctor can be a reasonable complementary option for certain concerns once a serious cause has been ruled out, but should not replace initial medical evaluation.

Understanding how homeopathic training and regulation actually work in India does not settle the separate question of homeopathy's clinical effectiveness, which the current evidence base remains limited and contested on. What it does offer is a clearer basis for choosing practitioners thoughtfully and knowing when a concern genuinely needs an MBBS doctor's evaluation first.
